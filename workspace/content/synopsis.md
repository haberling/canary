# Synopsis

## Act I: A rocky seashore on the coast of Cornwall

Frederic's twenty-first birthday has arrived, and with it his release from apprenticeship to the Pirates of Penzance — a band of pirates famous for being orphans themselves, and for that reason unable to bring themselves to harm anyone who claims to be an orphan too, a loophole their victims have caught on to. Frederic announces he intends to help exterminate his former crew, out of pure sense of duty, though he still feels an odd affection for them (and for Ruth, who has been his sole female company for most of his life, and hopes to marry him).

Wandering the shore, Frederic encounters Major-General Stanley's many daughters, and falls instantly for Mabel. When the pirates try to make off with the rest of the daughters, the Major-General arrives and — having no actual pirate blood but plenty of nerve — talks his way out of trouble by claiming to be an orphan himself. The tender-hearted pirates, bound by their own code, let the whole party go.

## Act II: A ruined chapel by moonlight

The Major-General is wracked with guilt over the lie. Meanwhile, Frederic prepares to lead the local police against his former crew, only for the Pirate King and Ruth to reveal the technicality at the heart of the plot: Frederic was born on **February 29th**, so he has, strictly speaking, only had five birthdays. His indenture doesn't expire until he turns twenty-one *in birthdays*, which won't happen again until 1940. Bound again by his own overdeveloped sense of duty, Frederic rejoins the pirates and, feeling honor-bound, reveals the Major-General's lie to the Pirate King.

A confrontation follows, resolved (as it must be) by the Sergeant of Police invoking Queen Victoria's name, and the final revelation that the pirates are, in fact, "noblemen who have gone wrong" — which, under the circumstances, means all is instantly forgiven, Frederic's birthday technicality is waved away, and the story ends in a general engagement of pirates to Major-General's daughters.

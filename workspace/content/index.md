# The Pirates of Penzance

*or, The Slave of Duty*

A comic operetta in two acts, music by Arthur Sullivan and libretto by W. S. Gilbert. It premiered in New York in December 1879, with the London premiere following in April 1880 — Gilbert and Sullivan staged the New York opening first specifically to secure the American copyright, since their previous hit, *H.M.S. Pinafore*, had been widely pirated by unlicensed American productions. There's a certain irony in a show about pirates being written partly as a legal maneuver against them.

## The premise

Frederic, apprenticed to a band of tender-hearted pirates as a boy by his nursemaid Ruth — who misheard his father's instruction to apprentice him to a *pilot* — has just turned twenty-one and is free to leave piracy behind. He promptly falls in love with Mabel, one of the many daughters of Major-General Stanley, and resolves to help wipe out his former crewmates. Complications, as always in Gilbert and Sullivan, follow from a technicality: Frederic was born on February 29th in a leap year, so by the strict letter of his indenture — which binds him only until his *twenty-first birthday* — he is technically still an apprentice for another six decades or so.
